Trade Policy and the Green Transition Monitor

Mapping the gap between environmental commitments and trade policy behaviour

This dashboard tracks how trade policy is shaping the global green transition. Its purpose is diagnostic: to assess the extent to which governments' trade and industrial policy choices support or undermine the shift away from fossil fuels. All dynamic indicators are derived directly from GTA intervention records and are refreshed daily. FTA environmental provision data is updated periodically.

Methodology

FTA Environmental Commitments

Static dataset based on CLAIRK's coding of environmental provisions in free trade agreements. A "country pair" is counted once per bilateral FTA, regardless of the number of provisions. Chord ribbon width is proportional to the number of environmental provisions in the agreement.

Net Policy Stance on Green Goods

Count of liberalising interventions (GTA evaluation = Green) minus harmful interventions (GTA evaluation = Red or Amber) on green goods HS codes, per implementing jurisdiction. Positive values indicate a net liberalising stance; negative values indicate a net harmful stance.

Green Sector Support Measures

Combines three intervention categories: (a) harmful subsidies (MAST Chapter L, Red or Amber) on green goods, hydrogen, and strategic minerals; (b) liberalising import barriers (tariffs, quotas, bans, licensing requirements) on the same products; (c) liberalising export controls on the same products. Counts are cumulative since January 2020.

Growth of Green Industrial Policy

Annual counts of all intervention types (both harmful and liberalising) affecting green goods, hydrogen, and strategic minerals HS codes, announced since 2020.

Active Fossil Fuel Support

Cumulative count of in-force subsidy interventions (MAST Chapter L) on coal, oil, gas, and petrochemicals HS codes per implementing jurisdiction, restricted to measures currently in force.

Fossil Fuel vs Green Subsidies

Annual counts of subsidy interventions (MAST Chapter L) on fossil fuel HS codes versus green goods, hydrogen, and strategic mineral HS codes, to illustrate the relative trajectory of public support.

Trade Remedies on Green Goods

Cumulative counts of anti-dumping, anti-subsidy, safeguard, and anti-circumvention interventions where at least one affected product falls within the green goods HS code list, per implementing jurisdiction.

Export Restrictions on Strategic Minerals

Annual counts of export control interventions (export bans, quotas, taxes, tariff quotas, licensing requirements, price benchmarks, and local supply requirements) on HS codes for lithium, copper, cobalt, nickel, graphite, and rare earths separately.

All dynamic indicators are computed from the Global Trade Alert database via the GTA API and refreshed daily. Intervention counts reflect announcements from 1 January 2020. HS 281410 appears in both the green goods and petrochemicals categories; interventions on this code contribute to both sets of indicators. GTA evaluations: Red = almost certainly harmful to foreign commercial interests; Amber = likely harmful; Green = almost certainly liberalising.
